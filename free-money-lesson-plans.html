What’s in the four weeks?

Week 1 · Wants, needs, and the bills speech

Read pages 1–3 · 45 min

Clarence earns a robot with chores and good grades. Then Mom lists what the house actually pays for. Kids open with a thumbs-up, thumbs-down vote on six items (the phone argument is worth letting run), then sort ten cards into needs and wants in pairs. Each pair defends the one card they almost moved.

Exit ticket: draw one need and one want. Label both.

Week 2 · Sale ads, coupons, and comparison shopping

Read pages 4–10 · 45 min

Hold up a Sunday flyer and ask what it’s for. Most kids say recycling. So did Clarence. Pairs hunt one item through real store flyers and the class picks the best price. Then coupon math:

One item, $4.00 sale price, three coupons.
25¢ off → $3.75 · 10% off → $3.60 · Buy one, get one → $4.00 for two, $2.00 each.
Which coupon saved the most? Kids are usually surprised.

Week 3 · Clearance, markdowns, and the Two-Robot Test

Read pages 11–22 · 45 min

The aisle-five chapter. Clearance means the store needs the shelf space. It doesn’t mean broken. Pairs inspect two classroom “robots,” one at $10 and one marked down to $8 with a clearance sticker, list the differences, and decide which one to buy with $10 of play money. Then they get the coupon Clarence had in his pocket.

10% off the $8 clearance price: $8.00 × 0.10 = $0.80 off → $7.20.
Then the register adds sales tax. That’s the second surprise at checkout.

Week 4 · Family money games and the post-test

Read pages 23–32 · 45 min

Wrap the book, give the post-assessment, then play Guess the Price with three real grocery items (closest guess wins; the highest guess has to explain themselves). Every kid leaves with a Wyze Shopper Certificate and one money habit to try at home: read a sale ad with the family, ask about a coupon at checkout, find a clearance sticker.

Yes. Each lesson carries separate notes for grades 1–2 and grades 3–5. First graders sort, vote, and argue about the phone. Third graders do the percent math. The book reads at an estimated Lexile AD 620L, guided reading L–N, and it’s a read-aloud anyway.

Why a picture book instead of a worksheet?

Because six-year-olds don’t remember worksheets. They remember the kid who wanted the robot. Every concept in this unit happens to Clarence first, on a real shopping trip, in order: the ad, the comparison, the markdown, the coupon, the tax. The lesson just slows the story down long enough to do the math.
